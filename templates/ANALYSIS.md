The turnover cap is the binding constraint: duration ends at {{r_dur}} against a target of {{b_dur}}. With a looser cap,
the remaining gap ({{durgap_after}} yrs) would close by selling long credit. Simulated costs are 8–22bp for corporates,

the benchmark). That is a deliberate, cost-aware choice and should be read as a spread view that remains in the book.

## 7. Model risk and limitations

* Corporate spreads, ratings, issuers and costs are synthetic. Corporate prices are model outputs, not quotes.
* The curve is built from constant-maturity par yields on a stylized grid, not from individual CUSIPs. There is no
  bill/coupon basis and no on/off-the-run premium.
* Shocks are instantaneous and spread shocks are parallel by rating tier. There is no default or migration risk, and no
  liquidity horizon.
* Historical VaR is first order and rates only. Parametric VaR assumes normal returns.
* The optimizer is a single-period LP with linear costs and no minimum trade size.
