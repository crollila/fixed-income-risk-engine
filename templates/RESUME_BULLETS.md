# Resume bullets: Fixed-Income Risk Engine

Each number is generated by `python -m firisk.run_all` (simulated portfolio, not real AUM).

* Built a from-scratch Python fixed-income pricing and risk engine (no QuantLib wrapping) covering ACT/ACT and 30/360
  accrual, coupon schedules, clean/dirty pricing, YTM, Treasury curve bootstrapping (par instruments reprice to par with error
  {{boot_err}}), spot/forward/discount curves, Macaulay/modified/effective duration, convexity, DV01, 11-bucket
  key-rate DV01, z-spread, spread duration and CS01.
* Analyzed a simulated {{p_nav_mm}} Treasury/IG-credit portfolio ({{n_held}} bonds, {{n_issuers}} issuers, {{n_sectors}}
  sectors) against a simulated 60/40 benchmark: duration {{p_dur}} vs {{b_dur}}, DV01 {{p_dv01}}/bp with the largest
  key-rate bucket at {{p_kr_top}} ({{p_kr_top_v}}/bp), CS01 {{p_cs01}}/bp. Key-rate buckets reconcile to parallel DV01
  within {{kr_recon}}.
* Ran 11 full-repricing stress scenarios (parallel ±25/50/100bp, steepener/flattener, tiered IG widening, combined
  rates+credit), with a worst case of {{worst_pnl}} ({{worst_pct}} NAV). Showed that convexity-adjusted
  approximations cut the worst error from {{max_err1_pct}} to {{max_err2_pct}} of P&L, and verified 2nd/3rd-order error
  convergence (fitted log-log slopes {{conv_slope1}} / {{conv_slope2}}).
* Ran PCA on {{pca_n}} days of FRED Treasury curve changes and identified level/slope/curvature empirically
  ({{ev1}}/{{ev2}}/{{ev3}} of variance). Mapped them onto key-rate DV01s for factor risk: PC1 is {{pc1_vshare}} of
  portfolio rate variance, parametric 99% 1-day VaR is {{var99}} and historical VaR is {{hvar99}}.
* Built a HiGHS linear-programming rebalancer with issuer/sector caps, a minimum Treasury allocation, a turnover limit
  and transaction costs. It cleared {{n_violations_before}} policy breaches, moved duration {{p_dur}}→{{r_dur}} and cut
  the key-rate tracking gap {{krgap_cut}} at {{turnover}} turnover and {{cost_bp}}bp cost, with every constraint
  verified independently after trade rounding.
* Shipped with {{n_tests}} pytest test functions against closed-form fixtures (zero-coupon identity, par-at-par, annuity pricing,
  analytic-vs-finite-difference DV01, accrual edge cases), an optional QuantLib cross-check, CI, pinned dependencies,
  an offline data cache, and README/memo numbers regenerated from program output.
